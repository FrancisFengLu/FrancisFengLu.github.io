About Me 
======

I am passionate about bridging **statistical genetics**, **bioinformatics**, **applied mathematics** and **GPU computing**, and I am exploring opportunities at their intersection. In particular, I am dedicated to bringing ideas from AI infrastructure and electrical engineering into statistical genetics, to enable a new era for the field.

I lead the development of **cuSAIGE** (to be generalized as **xSAIGE** to support hardware beyond NVIDIA GPUs), which runs SAIGE, a widely used mixed-model method for genome-wide association studies (GWAS), entirely on the GPU. It is about 3,400× faster than the original SAIGE for binary traits and 15,000× faster for quantitative traits, at two to three orders of magnitude lower cost, with no compromise in statistical accuracy. Analyses that were once too expensive to run, such as testing thousands of traits across an entire biobank, are now routine.

Few methods are designed with their statistics, approximations and engineering considered together. cuSAIGE shows how much this can unlock, and suggests that more sophisticated methods are within immediate reach. I want to design such methods, whose assumptions, approximations and computation are developed together, to uncover more of the genetic associations that precision medicine relies on.

**Please feel free to contact me for collaboration.**

My academic background is in **Applied Math/Statistics** and **Computer Science**, and I have trained in **Bioinformatics** at Massachusetts General Hospital.

This journey began in 2024 when, as an applied math student, I sought a research area that could fully leverage my interdisciplinary strengths. I was introduced to a project at the **Center for Functional Cancer Epigenetics (CFCE)**, focusing on clustering cell types using scATAC-seq data—a process strikingly similar to a final project I completed in a high-dimensional statistics course with Prof. Mauro Maggioni at Hopkins.

Through this experience, I came to appreciate the critical role of mathematical thinking in biomedical research—and the importance of making complex, often counterintuitive, high-dimensional concepts accessible to collaborators from diverse fields.
